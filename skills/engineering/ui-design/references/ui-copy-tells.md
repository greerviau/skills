# UI copy tells

Interface text reads as model-written when it would fit any product.
Replace each tell with a fact from the request, the product, or its data.
One tell proves little.
Repeats and clusters are the signal.

## Never use

- Hype words that stand in for a fact: seamless, effortless, powerful, robust, cutting-edge, next-generation, world-class, best-in-class, unlock, unleash, empower, elevate, supercharge, leverage, harness, revolutionary, transformative, game-changing.
  Replace "seamless" with the setup steps, "robust" with the failure it survives, "powerful" with the figure.
  A word that is literal in the product's domain stays (a climbing harness, financial leverage).
- Stock lines: "Unlock the power of", "X made simple", "Say goodbye to", "Whether you're A or B", "Join thousands of", "Trusted by teams worldwide", "Ready to transform your X?", "at your fingertips", "in just a few clicks", "Look no further", "The future of X is here".
- Residue: "Certainly!", `[Company Name]`, lorem ipsum, `{{tokens}}`, and markdown asterisks or `##` in rendered text.
- Invented specifics: figures, user counts, percentages, quotes, timestamps, and batch numbers that the request or data does not give.
  Without a source, write a plainer line, or a labeled placeholder in a mockup.
- The default cast in fixtures: Acme, Northwind, Contoso, Lumen, Nimbus, Apex, Zenith, Vertex, Kestrel, Halcyon, and Jane Doe, Priya, Marcus, Aisha, Maya, Elara.
  Use names that fit the product's users, and vary their length so long names test wrapping.
- Text that describes the interface: "This section shows", "Below, you'll find", "Here are 3 reasons", "We've added".

## At most once per screen

- Two-fragment headings: "Twelve rooms. Three ways to stay."
- Number-pair headings: "Four projects, four different problems."
- Contrast frames and their substitutes, counted together: "not X but Y", "rather than", "instead of", "without the".
- Negation runs: "No forms, no back-and-forth."
  Keep one only when each item is a claim the user can check.
- The trailing -ing clause: ", ensuring a smooth start".
- The reveal: "The result? Three hours back." and "One thing changed: X".
- Exclamation marks.

## Patterns

- Virtue words about the product: honest, simple, calm, thoughtful, intentional, genuinely, truly, actually.
  Show the quality with a fact.
- Every card and paragraph the same length and the same arc.
  Spend words where the product has the most to say.
- A quip or moral at the end of each paragraph.
  End on information.
- Default microcopy under hand-tuned headings.
  Write the error, empty, and confirmation text in the same voice.
- "Most popular" on the middle pricing tier, and three polished testimonials, without data behind them.

## Over-correction

Each rule above breeds a habit when applied mechanically.

| Rule | Habit it breeds | Counter |
| --- | --- | --- |
| No em dashes | Comma splices and fragments | Colons and parentheses where natural |
| No "not X but Y" | "rather than", "instead of", "without the" | State the positive fact alone |
| No hype words | Calm words: quietly, steady, honest, practical | A fact in place of the adjective |
| Avoid lists of three | Everything in pairs | List as many items as exist |
| Be specific | Invented numbers and anecdotes | Facts from the request, or a plainer line |
| Short sentences | A run of fragments | Let one sentence per section run long |
| Add personality | A joke in every section | Keep humour to microcopy |

## Not tells

Perfect grammar, plain "is" and "has", a list of three when there are three things, title case when it is the product's house style, and text the user wrote.
