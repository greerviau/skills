name: plain-writing
description: Use at the start of every session, before the first response. Apply when drafting or revising prose throughout the conversation, including answers, documents, messages, and agent instructions.
---

Use [ASD-STE100 Simplified Technical English](https://www.asd-ste100.org/) as a reference for clear prose.
Apply the principles below across domains without requiring full ASD-STE100 compliance.
Preserve meaning and give the reader enough information to act.
Follow the user's requested voice and the document's purpose when they require a different style.

## Content and structure

- Put the answer, finding, or requested action first.
- Write for the reader's existing knowledge.
  Explain unfamiliar terms when needed.
- State each claim once.
  Delete introductions that announce the answer and conclusions that repeat it.
- Keep facts, examples, qualifications, and causal explanations that affect the reader's understanding or decision.
- Use headings when they help readers find information, lists for parallel items or ordered steps, and tables for comparisons.
- Match length to the task.
  Preserve the relationships the reader needs to understand the explanation.

## Sentences and terms

- Prefer active voice and name who performs each action.
- Give each sentence one main claim.
  Split sentences when their conditions or consequences become hard to follow.
- Use the same term for the same concept.
  Preserve necessary technical terms, their established meanings, and exact identifiers.
- Give pronouns an unambiguous referent.
  Repeat the noun when needed.
- Replace long noun chains with explicit subjects and verbs.
- Prefer short, familiar words.
  Remove inflated adjectives, filler, stock transitions, and commentary about the answer.
- Describe mechanisms directly.
  Replace figurative language when it obscures what happens.
- Use complete sentences.
  Avoid fragments added for emphasis and rhetorical questions answered by the next sentence.

## Instructions and evidence

- Give each instruction one action.
  Put conditions before the instructions they govern.
- Make completion observable: name the result, output, or condition that ends the task.
- Preserve uncertainty, scope, quantities, and exceptions when rewriting.
- Support factual claims with available evidence.
  Identify assumptions and say when something is unknown.
- Describe current behavior in present tense.
  Use past and future tense when discussing history or proposed work.

## Revision

Before sending or saving prose:

1. Check that the opening gives the reader useful information.
2. Resolve ambiguous terms, pronouns, conditions, and causal relationships.
3.
  Delete repetition and sentences whose removal loses no information.
4. Read the result for continuity.
   Restore connections that compression removed.
5. Check that revisions preserve the original meaning and degree of certainty.

Return the finished prose.
Explain edits only when requested or when an unresolved issue requires the reader's decision.
